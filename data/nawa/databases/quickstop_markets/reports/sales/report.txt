========================================
EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
========================================

QuickStop Markets closed the first quarter of 2024 with consistent revenue growth across both in-store merchandise and fuel operations, driven by stronger basket sizes in March and steady loyalty programme engagement. Across five active store locations spanning the Puget Sound and Willamette Valley regions, in-store transaction volumes and average transaction values both trended upward through the quarter, with March recording the highest weekly fuel gallons sold at fuel-enabled locations.

Tobacco and Vape remained the highest revenue-generating product category in absolute terms, led by cigarette and disposable vape pen sales, though the Fresh Deli category demonstrated strong margin expansion relative to its category share. Beverages continued to anchor basket-level attachment across all stores, appearing in a large proportion of multi-item transactions.

Store 1 (Bellevue Central) and Store 4 (Portland East) emerged as the top-performing locations overall when combining in-store and fuel revenue. Loyalty-enrolled customers accounted for a majority of identified card transactions and demonstrated materially higher average transaction values than anonymous shoppers.

As QuickStop heads into Q2, opportunities exist to deepen fresh category attachment, optimise fuel margin in a rising cost environment, and address inventory pressure emerging at several locations.

SECTION 1: OVERALL REVENUE PERFORMANCE BY MONTH
========================================

Reviewing in-store transactions by month reveals a clear upward trajectory across Q1 2024. January established the baseline with 10 recorded transactions across all five stores, yielding moderate average basket sizes. February showed increased engagement from loyalty customers and higher-value category purchases. March demonstrated the strongest performance, with single-transaction totals reaching as high as $34.94.

This month-over-month growth pattern is consistent with seasonal foot traffic improvement as weather conditions improve across the Pacific Northwest in late Q1.

Data fields used: transactions.transaction_date, transactions.total_amount, transactions.store_id, transactions.payment_method

========================================
SECTION 2: REVENUE BREAKDOWN BY PRODUCT CATEGORY
========================================

Tobacco & Vape generated the largest revenue contribution, driven by high unit prices for cigarette packs ($9.99) and disposable vape pens ($12.99). These items frequently appeared alongside Beverages, suggesting a habitual co-purchase pattern worth reinforcing through shelf placement strategy.

Fresh Deli ranked second in revenue and delivered the highest gross margins — the Turkey Deli Sandwich and Breakfast Burrito both carry margins above 60%. Beverages performed as a consistent basket-builder category, appearing in nearly every multi-item transaction. Snacks & Candy and Household Essentials served as supplementary attachment items that lifted transaction values without dominating any single purchase.

Data fields used: transaction_items.product_id, transaction_items.line_total, transaction_items.unit_price, transaction_items.unit_cost, products.product_name, product_categories.category_name, product_categories.department

SECTION 3: STORE-LEVEL PERFORMANCE COMPARISON
========================================

Store 1 (Bellevue Central) led all locations in both transaction volume and total revenue during Q1, benefiting from its established customer base and fuel capability. Store 4 (Portland East) ranked closely behind when fuel revenue is included, showing strong Tobacco & Vape and Household Essentials sales. Store 2 (Seattle Pike) maintained solid in-store performance despite lacking fuel services. Store 3 (Tacoma South) demonstrated healthy Fresh Deli and Beverage sales. Store 5 (Eugene Main), the most recently opened location, showed lower overall volume consistent with its maturity stage.

Stores without fuel are structurally limited in total revenue potential and may benefit from accelerated fresh and specialty category expansion to offset this gap.

Data fields used: stores.store_id, stores.store_name, stores.city, stores.region, stores.has_fuel, transactions.store_id, transactions.total_amount, transactions.transaction_date

========================================
SECTION 4: FUEL SALES ANALYSIS — VOLUME, MIX, AND MARGIN
========================================

Fuel operations at Stores 1, 3, and 4 generated substantial revenue across Q1. Store 1 (Bellevue Central) recorded the highest total fuel revenue, driven by both high Regular-grade volume and a meaningful Premium grade mix. Regular gasoline accounted for the largest share of gallons sold, consistent with the Pacific Northwest commuter demographic. Diesel sales occurred consistently across all fuel weeks, indicating a reliable commercial vehicle customer segment.

Price-per-gallon for all fuel types trended upward across the quarter — Regular rising from approximately $3.44 to $3.60 — reflecting regional wholesale price movements. Margin per gallon remained relatively stable. Premium grade carried the highest retail price and per-gallon margin, representing a revenue quality opportunity if mix can be shifted through forecourt signage or loyalty incentives.

========================================
SECTION 6: PAYMENT METHOD AND TRANSACTION BEHAVIOUR
========================================

Card payments accounted for approximately 77% of all Q1 in-store transactions. Cash usage was more prevalent at Stores 3 and 5, reflecting local demographic and payment habit differences. Card-paying customers tended to record higher transaction totals, consistent with the loyalty programme's card-first structure. Anonymous cash transactions typically comprised one to three low-cost items with below-average basket values.

This pattern supports an initiative to incentivise card payment adoption at cash-heavy locations, which could simultaneously drive loyalty enrolment and basket value improvement.

Data fields used: transactions.payment_method, transactions.total_amount, transactions.customer_id, transactions.store_id, transactions.transaction_date

APPENDIX AND NOTES
========================================

ASSUMPTIONS
- Transaction records represent a representative sample; full-quarter totals in production would be materially larger.
- Fuel sales records are aggregated on a weekly basis per store rather than individual fill-up transactions.
- Loyalty point balances are cumulative as of the data extraction date and are not delineated by quarter.
- Margin calculations assume no promotional discounting was applied during the period.
- Stores 2 and 5 have no fuel sales records, consistent with the has_fuel flag in the stores table.

KNOWN LIMITATIONS
- The 30 in-store transaction sample is representative; a live production environment would contain significantly more records for the full quarter.
- No returns, refunds, or voided transactions are captured; gross revenue figures may be marginally overstated.
- Fuel margin analysis does not account for delivery fees, blending costs, or environmental compliance costs.
- Customer demographic segmentation is limited to enrolment status and accumulated loyalty points.
- Daypart analysis (morning vs. afternoon vs. evening traffic) has not been fully developed in this report.

RECOMMENDED NEXT ANALYSIS
- Daypart transaction analysis to identify peak traffic windows per store and optimise staffing and fresh product availability.
- Loyalty redemption forecasting based on current point balances to anticipate Q2 margin impact.
- Category cross-purchase affinity analysis to identify optimal product adjacency and shelf placement strategies.
- Fuel margin sensitivity modelling to assess profitability impact of continued wholesale price increases.
- Non-enrolled customer conversion analysis targeting cash-paying anonymous shoppers with loyalty enrolment incentives.
- Store 5 (Eugene Main) growth trajectory assessment relative to peer stores at equivalent maturity.
