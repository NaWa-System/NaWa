Q1 2024 INVENTORY HEALTH & DAYS-IN-STOCK ANALYSIS
Meridian Auto Group
Reporting Period: January 1, 2024 – March 31, 2024
Prepared by: Business Intelligence & Reporting Team
Date Issued: April 2024

========================================
EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
========================================

This report evaluates the inventory health of Meridian Auto Group's five-location network over the first quarter of 2024, drawing on end-of-month inventory snapshots, sold vehicle days-in-inventory data, and segment-level stock distribution. The analysis is intended to support operational decisions around pricing, sourcing, and lot management heading into Q2.

Across all locations and segments, average days-in-stock showed a modest but consistent improvement between January and March, declining from a network average of approximately 26 days at January month-end to approximately 22.5 days at March month-end. This trend reflects both stronger retail demand in March and incremental pricing discipline by the field teams. However, the Premium segment continues to carry meaningfully higher average days-in-stock relative to Economy and Mid-Range.

Inventory depth at the end of Q1 shows adequate supply across Economy and Mid-Range segments, though Chattanooga and Nashville are approaching minimum Economy stock thresholds. Premium inventory at Chattanooga is especially thin at just two units, potentially limiting Q2 conversion opportunities.

Sold vehicle data confirms an inverse relationship between holding time and realised margin. Units sold within 20 days achieved near-list pricing, while those held beyond 30 days required discounting. Management is advised to adopt a 45-day maximum holding target, with escalating markdown authority triggers at days 30 and 45.

========================================
SECTION 1: INVENTORY DEPTH BY LOCATION AND SEGMENT (END OF QUARTER)
========================================

At March month-end, the network held 73 total units. Atlanta North carried the largest inventory, followed by Birmingham. Economy and Mid-Range were adequately stocked at most locations, but Premium inventory was thin at Chattanooga (2 units) and Birmingham (3 units). A minimum of 3 to 4 Premium units per active location is recommended to sustain conversion opportunity.



========================================
SECTION 2: DAYS-IN-STOCK TREND BY MONTH
========================================

End-of-month average days-in-stock declined across the quarter for Economy and Mid-Range at most locations. Atlanta North showed the most improvement in Mid-Range (19.2 days in January to 17.5 days in March). Premium days-in-stock remained elevated and showed minimal improvement. Chattanooga Premium at 32.5 average days in March is the highest single segment-location figure and warrants a pricing review.



========================================
SECTION 3: SOLD VEHICLE DAYS-IN-INVENTORY VS. PRICE REALISATION
========================================

Vehicles sold within 20 days of arriving on lot sold at or near list price. Units held beyond 30 days — including the Chevrolet Malibu (45 days), Nissan Altima (38 days), Ram 1500 (37 days), and Chevrolet Equinox (41 days) — sold below list price, reducing gross profit. Implementing tiered markdown triggers at day 30 and day 45 is recommended to protect margin while improving turnover.



========================================
SECTION 4: CERTIFIED PRE-OWNED VS. NON-CERTIFIED INVENTORY PERFORMANCE
========================================

CPO vehicles (12 of 20 sold) demonstrated faster average days-in-inventory than non-certified equivalents. The Mercedes-Benz C-Class CPO sold in just 9 days. Non-certified units including the Ford F-150 and Jeep Grand Cherokee took 31 and 27 days respectively. CPO certification also supported higher F&I product attachment. Meridian should prioritise CPO conversion for eligible incoming trade-in units.



========================================
SECTION 5: INVENTORY REDUCTION OVER QUARTER
========================================

Network inventory reduced from approximately 93 units at January month-end to 73 units at March month-end — a net reduction of 20 units, consistent with the 20 vehicles sold in Q1. This indicates no net restocking occurred during the period. Without active Q2 acquisition activity, stock levels may fall below optimal retail depth, particularly in Mid-Range and Premium at Chattanooga and Birmingham.



========================================
SECTION 6: SEGMENT MIX AND STRATEGIC SOURCING IMPLICATIONS
========================================

March ending inventory skews toward Mid-Range (approximately 49 units), with Economy at approximately 22 and Premium at approximately 15. Given Premium units' gross profit contribution and CPO certification advantages, the current balance may underweight Premium relative to margin opportunity. Q2 sourcing should target late-model luxury vehicles aged 1 to 3 years with under 60,000 miles, especially for Chattanooga and Birmingham where current Premium stock is minimal.



========================================
APPENDIX & NOTES
========================================

ASSUMPTIONS
- Inventory snapshot data represents end-of-month positions and is used as the basis for all trend analysis.
- Total network inventory is the sum of units_available across all location-segment rows per snapshot date.
- Days-in-inventory for sold units is taken from the sales.days_in_inventory field at point of sale.
- CPO status is determined by the vehicles.certified field.
- No net new acquisitions are assumed to have been recorded during Q1, as total inventory movement equals units sold.

KNOWN LIMITATIONS
- Monthly snapshots do not capture intra-month stock movements or transfers between locations.
- Individual vehicle arrival dates are not recorded for current on-lot inventory, limiting precise aging analysis.
- Reconditioning timelines are not tracked, preventing analysis of acquisition-to-lot delay.
- List price is used as the published retail benchmark without adjustment for market repricing events.
- Five locations and three months of data represent a relatively short window for establishing reliable seasonal benchmarks.

RECOMMENDED NEXT ANALYSIS
- Implement individual vehicle arrival date tracking to enable precise aging analysis for all on-lot inventory.
- Analyse reconditioning throughput time and cost by vehicle make and condition.
- Model the gross margin impact of a 45-day lot rotation policy with day-30 and day-45 markdown triggers.
- Track CPO certification conversion rate from acquired trade-ins to quantify programme effectiveness.
- Build a Q2 sourcing plan aligned to segment-level inventory gaps, with target acquisition volumes and price ceilings by segment.
