# Q1 2026 Transport Performance & Customer Service Review

**Organisation:** NordHearth Scania Fleet Services  
**Reporting period:** 2026-01-01 to 2026-03-31

## Executive Summary
During Q1 2026, NordHearth Scania Fleet Services maintained steady contract activity across five core customers with a balanced mix of per-trip and per-mile pricing. Delivered shipments formed the clear majority of loads for the quarter, with a small number of late deliveries concentrated on longer-haul per-mile lanes and one cancelled movement late in March.

On-time performance was generally strong on metro-to-regional routes where planned buffers are well understood. Variance increased on multi-state runs, where weather and dock congestion increased schedule pressure and created late-delivery exposure. Fuel cost remained a material driver of margin variability; routes with higher miles per load showed visibly higher fuel spend and more sensitivity to delivery windows.

Customer concentration remained moderate, with NorthStar Manufacturing and Lakefront Retail Distribution representing a large portion of revenue-generating activity. This concentration is beneficial for planning density, but it also elevates service-level expectations and highlights the importance of reliability and proactive communication.

Recommended actions for Q2 include tightening lane-level planning assumptions for the longest per-mile lanes, standardizing late-delivery root-cause capture in shipment closeout, and coordinating maintenance scheduling to reduce unplanned disruptions during peak ship weeks.

## Main Sections
### 1) Shipment Volume & Status Mix
Q1 activity showed consistent movement and a strong delivered mix. Exceptions were limited, but their timing indicates the need for better contingency capacity during weeks with both longer lanes and maintenance activity.

### 2) Revenue and Fuel Cost Overview
Revenue tracked with distance and shipment volume, while fuel costs rose more sharply on long-haul per-mile runs. Margin variability appears lane-driven and should be addressed with lane-tier planning and pricing checks.

### 3) On-Time Performance and Late Delivery Review
Late deliveries were concentrated in longer-distance schedules. Improving lane buffers and applying proactive customer notifications for high-risk routes should reduce perceived variability.

### 4) Customer Activity and Concentration
NorthStar Manufacturing and Lakefront Retail Distribution drive a meaningful share of activity. Service stability for these accounts remains the highest priority, while selective diversification would reduce reliance risk.

### 5) Region and Lane Characteristics
Metro lanes showed better schedule stability, while cross-state runs introduced higher variability and fuel sensitivity. This supports tracking KPIs by lane tier rather than rolling all shipments into a single operational baseline.

### 6) Driver and Vehicle Utilization (High-Level)
Utilization is spread across five drivers and multiple Scania units with recurring lane assignments. Vehicles carrying the most long-haul miles should be prioritized for preventive maintenance planning to avoid disruptive repairs.

## Appendix / Notes
**Assumptions**
- On-time delivery is inferred from shipment status.
- Gross margin is approximated as revenue minus fuel cost.
- Q1 includes shipment_date from 2026-01-01 to 2026-03-31.

**Known Limitations**
- Accessorials, tolls, and detention are not captured.
- Cancellation reasons are not recorded.
- Driver hours and HOS/compliance metrics are not included.

**Recommended Next Analysis**
- Add lane master data and planned vs actual transit time.
- Track accessorials to refine route profitability.
- Add exception reason codes for late/cancelled shipments.
