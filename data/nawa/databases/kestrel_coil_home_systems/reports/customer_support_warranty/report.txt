Q1 2026 Warranty & Service Operations Review (Jan–Mar 2026)

Organisation: Kestrel & Coil Home Systems
Reporting Period: 2026-01-01 to 2026-03-31

EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
Service activity in Q1 was concentrated in February and March, largely tied to higher-volume categories sold through wholesale partners and a smaller number of higher-severity cases in refrigeration and dishwashing. Most tickets in the quarter were resolved or closed within a single-digit day range, indicating a generally effective support workflow when parts are readily available.

Leak and cooling-related issues are the most operationally material themes: leaks drive higher labor intensity in dishwashing, while cooling incidents in refrigeration have elevated severity and a higher customer impact risk. Open tickets at quarter-end were present, including a high-severity cooling ticket, suggesting a need to strengthen escalation paths and parts availability for the refrigeration platform.

Warranty coverage accounted for the majority of ticket volume, with one non-warranty installation-related ticket showing that customer expectation management and installer alignment can meaningfully reduce avoidable service cost.

The data supports prioritizing (1) dishwasher leak root-cause reduction, (2) refrigeration cooling escalation/parts readiness, and (3) installation guidance improvements for retail and installer channels to reduce non-warranty service interactions.

FINDINGS AND ANALYSIS
1) Ticket Volume Trend and Status Mix
Ticket volume increased into February and March, consistent with the ramp in delivered units from early-quarter wholesale and installer activity. Backlog remained low, but a single high-severity open case highlights the need for escalation discipline.

Recommendation: implement explicit SLA targets and escalation triggers for high severity categories.

2) Issues by Product and Category
Dishwashers are associated with leak incidents that are labor intensive. Refrigerators show cooling incidents with elevated severity. Other categories appear more sporadic.

Recommendation: run targeted reliability workstreams for dishwasher leak prevention and refrigerator cooling diagnostics/parts readiness.

3) Resolution Time Performance
Closed/resolved tickets typically completed within a week; longer durations correlate with higher severity and categories requiring more on-site work.

Recommendation: standardize parts kits for common failure modes and escalate high-severity cooling cases earlier.

4) Warranty vs. Non-Warranty Cost Drivers
Warranty-covered tickets drive the bulk of costs. A non-warranty installation ticket indicates preventable service interactions when installation responsibilities and first-use setup are unclear.

Recommendation: tighten installation documentation, partner guidance, and first-use customer education.

5) Regional Service Patterns
Tickets occurred across multiple regions with no single-region concentration. Variation may reflect partner installation practices and local servicing capacity.

Recommendation: align partner guidance and first-install checklists consistently by region.

6) Service Cost Snapshot (Parts and Labor)
Labor dominates recorded service cost in Q1, indicating that reducing truck rolls and on-site time is likely the most impactful lever. Parts costs are modest.

Recommendation: strengthen remote triage, improve setup instructions, and pre-position kits for high-frequency categories.
