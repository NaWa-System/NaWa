# Q1 2026 Production Efficiency & Yield Report (Plants and Product Mix)

**Organisation:** Northforge Steelworks AB  
**Reporting period:** 2026-01-01 to 2026-03-31

## Executive Summary
During Q1 2026, Northforge Steelworks AB maintained stable output across the strip/plate and downstream coating routes, with production spread across all three plants and a balanced mix of hot-rolled coils, plate, and coated products. Overall yield performance was consistent month to month, with scrap remaining a small share of total produced tons in most batches, indicating good process control and limited rework.

Energy usage tracked the product mix and processing route: the melt and strip operations recorded higher energy per batch than downstream coating/finishing, as expected. The strip mill ran frequent HRC campaigns (S450MC and S700MC), while the plate works produced fewer but heavier batches with steady scrap rates.

A notable operational risk entering Q2 is the late-March coated product volume that remained on quality hold, which may constrain April fulfillment unless disposition actions (release, downgrade, or rework) are executed quickly. Aligning quality release timing with shipment commitments should remain a priority to avoid cost increases from expediting or partial shipments.

Operational opportunities include continued yield tightening on high-strength HRC (S700MC) campaigns and targeted energy optimization on high-tonnage strip batches where incremental savings scale quickly.

## Main Sections
### 1) Production Volume Trend (Jan–Mar) by Plant
Quarterly output was anchored by the strip mill’s recurring HRC campaigns, with plate batches providing steady base-load tonnage and coating/finishing adding downstream volume in February and March. The distribution of production across sites supports resilience, but also highlights the need to coordinate downstream quality release with shipment timing to prevent bottlenecks at quarter-end.

### 2) Yield and Scrap Performance by Product Category
Scrap remained controlled across the quarter, with most released batches showing low scrapped tons relative to produced tons. Variation by product type is present, but the overall pattern indicates predictable process stability. Continued focus should be placed on high-strength HRC campaigns where small yield improvements create outsized quarterly benefit.

### 3) Energy Consumption and Estimated Energy Intensity
Energy use tracks processing route and campaign tonnage. Quarter-level energy intensity appears stable and consistent with the product mix. Targeted initiatives should prioritize the highest-tonnage strip campaigns for efficiency gains while preserving quality and throughput.

### 4) Product Mix: Output by Category and Key Grades
The quarter’s output reflects a balanced mix: HRC provides the largest tonnage base, plate supports heavy fabrication demand, and coated products supply downstream applications. Maintaining flexibility across these categories is beneficial, but the late-quarter quality hold in coated output underscores the need for robust quality gating and rapid disposition processes to protect service levels.

### 5) Quality Holds and Operational Risk to Early Q2
One coated batch remained on quality hold at quarter-end, creating potential short-term constraints for April deliveries if that volume was planned for open customer commitments. Priority actions are to accelerate testing/decisioning and to pre-plan contingency allocations from released production.

## Appendix / Notes
**Assumptions**
- Energy intensity is calculated as total batch energy divided by total produced tons for Q1.
- Scrap rate is estimated from total scrapped tons divided by total produced tons.
- Released batches are treated as available; quality-hold batches are treated as unavailable.
