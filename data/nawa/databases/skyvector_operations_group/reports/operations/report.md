# Q1 2024 Airline Operations Performance Report

**Organisation:** SkyVector Operations Group  
**Reporting Period:** January 1, 2024 – March 31, 2024  
**Report Type:** Operations  
**Prepared by:** SkyVector Analytics & Performance Team  
**Date of Issue:** April 5, 2024  

---

## Executive Summary

SkyVector Operations Group managed a total of 28 dispatched flights across four client airlines during the first quarter of 2024. The quarter presented a mixed operational picture: while a majority of flights operated on time or within acceptable tolerances, a recurring pattern of mechanical-category delays was the most significant driver of departure degradation across the portfolio, accounting for the highest average delay minutes and the greatest share of irregularity event cost impact.

On-time performance (OTP), defined as departure within 15 minutes of schedule, stood at approximately 57% across all client operations for the quarter. This figure is below the industry benchmark of 78–82% for regional carriers and warrants focused attention in Q2. Maverick Air recorded the highest volume of flights and the widest range of delay categories, including both mechanical and weather events. Saltline Express demonstrated the most consistent on-time operations across its six dispatched flights.

Irregularity events resulted in a combined estimated cost impact exceeding $44,000 for the quarter, with mechanical delays alone accounting for more than 70% of that figure. The most impactful single event was a pre-departure engine fault on a Maverick Air Boeing 737-700 in January, which required an aircraft swap and generated approximately $14,200 in direct remediation costs.

Crew-related events, while fewer in number, contributed meaningfully to departure delays. SkyVector's crew coordination teams responded effectively with substitution actions in all cases, but the underlying causes point to systemic crew scheduling vulnerabilities that should be reviewed with affected clients.

## 1. Overall Flight Operations Summary

A total of 28 revenue flights were dispatched across Maverick Air (10 flights), Pacific Crest Airways (6 flights), Saltline Express (6 flights), and Gulf Palms Charter (6 flights) during Q1 2024. All flights reached a Completed status, indicating no full cancellations within the reporting period. Flight volume was highest in January and March, consistent with seasonal demand patterns. SkyVector's operations center maintained continuous dispatch coverage across all four client hubs — DFW, SEA, SLC, and MIA — without any documented coverage gap events.

---

## 2. On-Time Performance by Client Airline

OTP varied significantly across client airlines. Saltline Express led the group with four of six flights departing on time (67% OTP). Gulf Palms Charter achieved a similar rate with three on-time departures from six flights. Maverick Air recorded the weakest OTP, with five of ten flights experiencing delays, including two events exceeding 70 minutes. Pacific Crest Airways had two on-time flights of six dispatched. Across the full portfolio, consistent underperformance against the 80% industry benchmark suggests systemic factors are suppressing fleet-wide OTP.

---

## 3. Delay Category Analysis

Mechanical issues were the dominant delay category for the quarter, appearing in five separate events with an average delay of approximately 57 minutes. Weather-related delays affected four flights with an average delay of 30 minutes. Air Traffic Control restrictions accounted for two events with relatively minor average delays of around 13 minutes. Crew late arrival contributed two events averaging 24 minutes. All mechanical events had associated irregularity cost records, supporting the case for enhanced pre-departure maintenance checks.

---

## 4. Irregularity Event Log and Cost Impact

Twelve irregularity events were formally logged and resolved during Q1. Mechanical delay events collectively generated $34,200 in estimated cost impact (77.6% of total). The highest-cost event was the Maverick Air MV201 engine bleed air fault ($14,200), followed by the Pacific Crest hydraulic warning ($8,750) and the Saltline Express APU failure ($6,300). Weather events contributed $4,500; ATC restrictions and crew events added $1,650 and $3,700 respectively. All events were resolved and aircraft returned to service within the same duty day.

---

## 5. Crew Assignment and Duty Compliance

Twenty crew assignments were logged across the period. One Late Start classification was applied to a Pacific Crest first officer on flight PC301, contributing to an 18-minute departure delay. All other assignments reached Completed status. Duty period extensions were authorised on at least two occasions. No duty time limit violations were identified. SkyVector's crew desk is commended for rapid substitution response on both late-crew events.

---

## 6. Fleet Status and Aircraft Utilisation

Eleven aircraft were tracked during Q1. One aircraft — Maverick Air N404MV — remained in maintenance status and was not dispatched. Boeing 737-800 frames operated the highest dispatch frequency. The Cessna Citation X (N702GP) completed two charter flights for Gulf Palms. The grounding of N404MV had no material impact on Q1 operations due to sufficient fleet redundancy, but a return-to-service date should be confirmed ahead of Q2.

---

## 7. Route and Hub Activity

SkyVector managed flights across ten airports during Q1. Dallas Fort Worth (DFW) was the most active origin hub. Denver (DEN), Las Vegas (LAS), and Chicago O'Hare (ORD) were the most frequently served destinations. The distribution of delays was not concentrated on any single route corridor, suggesting delay drivers are airline-fleet-specific rather than route-specific.

---

## 8. Month-over-Month Operational Trends

January 2024 was the most operationally turbulent month, recording the highest-cost mechanical event and the greatest number of weather irregularities. February continued to carry mechanical delay risk. March demonstrated modest OTP improvement. If this positive trajectory continues with enhanced maintenance pre-checks, OTP recovery toward 65–70% across the portfolio by mid-year is a reasonable projection.

## Appendix

### Assumptions
- OTP defined as actual departure within 15 minutes of scheduled departure.
- Irregularity cost figures are estimates subject to final invoice reconciliation.
- Aircraft in Maintenance status are excluded from utilisation calculations.
- All timestamps recorded in local time at origin airport.

### Known Limitations
- Crew assignment data covers a representative sample, not all operating crew members.
- Full load factor analysis is limited by partial passengers_boarded data.
- Cost impact data unavailable for ATC restrictions not escalating to a formal irregularity record.
- Weather classifications have not been cross-referenced against third-party meteorological sources.

### Recommended Next Analysis
- Root cause analysis on all five mechanical events cross-referenced against aircraft maintenance logs.
- Q2 OTP projection modelling under alternative maintenance protocol scenarios.
- Crew late-arrival pattern analysis against scheduling records.
- Cost-of-delay attribution model for monthly client reporting.
- Return-to-service assessment for Maverick Air N404MV ahead of summer peak.
