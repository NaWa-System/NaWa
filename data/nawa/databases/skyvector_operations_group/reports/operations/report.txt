Organisation:     SkyVector Operations Group

Report Type:      Operations
Prepared by:      SkyVector Analytics & Performance Team
Date of Issue:    April 5, 2024


EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
------------------

SkyVector Operations Group managed a total of 28 dispatched flights across four client airlines during the first quarter of 2024. The quarter presented a mixed operational picture: while a majority of flights operated on time or within acceptable tolerances, a recurring pattern of mechanical-category delays was the most significant driver of departure degradation across the portfolio, accounting for the highest average delay minutes and the greatest share of irregularity event cost impact.

On-time performance (OTP), defined as departure within 15 minutes of schedule, stood at approximately 57% across all client operations for the quarter. This figure is below the industry benchmark of 78-82% for regional carriers and warrants focused attention in Q2. Maverick Air recorded the highest volume of flights and the widest range of delay categories. Saltline Express demonstrated the most consistent on-time operations across its six dispatched flights.

Irregularity events resulted in a combined estimated cost impact exceeding $44,000 for the quarter, with mechanical delays alone accounting for more than 70% of that figure. The most impactful single event was a pre-departure engine fault on a Maverick Air Boeing 737-700 in January, which required an aircraft swap and generated approximately $14,200 in direct remediation costs.

Crew-related events, while fewer in number, contributed meaningfully to departure delays. SkyVector's crew coordination teams responded effectively with substitution actions in all cases, but the underlying causes point to systemic crew scheduling vulnerabilities that should be reviewed with affected clients.

SECTION 1: OVERALL FLIGHT OPERATIONS SUMMARY
---------------------------------------------

A total of 28 revenue flights were dispatched across Maverick Air (10 flights), Pacific Crest Airways (6 flights), Saltline Express (6 flights), and Gulf Palms Charter (6 flights) during Q1 2024. All flights reached a Completed status, indicating no full cancellations within the reporting period. Flight volume was highest in January and March, consistent with seasonal demand patterns. SkyVector's operations center maintained continuous dispatch coverage across all four client hubs - DFW, SEA, SLC, and MIA - without any documented coverage gap events.


SECTION 2: ON-TIME PERFORMANCE BY CLIENT AIRLINE
-------------------------------------------------

OTP varied significantly across client airlines. Saltline Express led the group with four of six flights departing on time (67% OTP), the strongest of the four clients. Gulf Palms Charter achieved a similar rate with three on-time departures from six flights. Maverick Air recorded the weakest OTP, with five of ten flights experiencing delays, including two events exceeding 70 minutes. Pacific Crest Airways had two on-time flights of six dispatched.

Across the full portfolio, consistent underperformance against the 80% industry benchmark suggests systemic factors are suppressing fleet-wide OTP. The operations team should prioritise targeted improvement plans with Maverick Air and Pacific Crest Airways for Q2.


SECTION 3: DELAY CATEGORY ANALYSIS
------------------------------------

Mechanical issues were the dominant delay category for the quarter, appearing in five events with an average delay of approximately 57 minutes - the highest of all categories. Weather-related delays affected four flights with an average delay of 30 minutes, concentrated in January and early March. Air Traffic Control restrictions accounted for two events with relatively minor average delays of around 13 minutes. Crew late arrival contributed two events averaging 24 minutes.

All mechanical events had associated irregularity cost records, supporting the case for enhanced pre-departure maintenance checks, particularly for older Embraer E175 frames in the Saltline and Pacific Crest fleets.


SECTION 4: IRREGULARITY EVENT LOG AND COST IMPACT
--------------------------------------------------

Twelve irregularity events were formally logged and resolved during Q1. Mechanical delay events collectively generated $34,200 in estimated cost impact (77.6% of total irregularity costs). The highest-cost event was the Maverick Air MV201 engine bleed air fault ($14,200), followed by the Pacific Crest Airways hydraulic warning ($8,750) and the Saltline Express APU failure ($6,300). Weather events contributed $4,500 combined. ATC restrictions and crew events added $1,650 and $3,700 respectively. All events were resolved and aircraft returned to service within the same duty day.


SECTION 5: CREW ASSIGNMENT AND DUTY COMPLIANCE
-----------------------------------------------

Twenty crew assignments were logged across the reporting period covering captain, first officer, and lead flight attendant roles. One Late Start classification was applied to a Pacific Crest Airways first officer on flight PC301 (February 7), contributing to an 18-minute departure delay. All other assignments reached Completed status. Duty period extensions were authorised on at least two occasions with proper operational authorisation. No duty time limit violations were identified. SkyVector's crew desk is commended for rapid substitution response on both late-crew events.


SECTION 6: FLEET STATUS AND AIRCRAFT UTILISATION
-------------------------------------------------

Eleven aircraft across four client airlines were tracked in the SkyVector platform during Q1. One aircraft - Maverick Air Embraer E175 (N404MV) - remained in maintenance status for the entire quarter and was not available for dispatch. Boeing 737-800 frames operated by Maverick Air and Pacific Crest recorded the highest dispatch frequency. The Cessna Citation X (N702GP) completed two charter flights for Gulf Palms. The N404MV grounding had no material impact on Q1 operations, but a return-to-service date should be confirmed ahead of Q2 summer scheduling.


SECTION 7: ROUTE AND HUB ACTIVITY
-----------------------------------

SkyVector managed flights across ten airports during Q1, with Dallas Fort Worth (DFW) serving as the most active origin hub. Denver (DEN), Las Vegas (LAS), and Chicago O'Hare (ORD) were the most frequently served destinations. No route was cancelled outright during Q1. The distribution of delays was not concentrated on any single route corridor, suggesting delay drivers are airline-fleet-specific rather than route-specific.


SECTION 8: MONTH-OVER-MONTH OPERATIONAL TRENDS
-----------------------------------------------

January 2024 was the most operationally turbulent month, recording the highest-cost mechanical event and the greatest number of weather irregularities, likely reflecting winter storm activity. February continued to carry mechanical delay risk across two clients. March demonstrated modest OTP improvement. If this positive trajectory continues with enhanced maintenance pre-checks, OTP recovery toward 65-70% across the managed portfolio by mid-year is a reasonable projection.


APPENDIX
--------

Assumptions:
  - OTP is defined as actual departure within 15 minutes of scheduled departure time.
  - Irregularity cost impact figures are estimates subject to final invoice reconciliation.
  - Aircraft in Maintenance status are excluded from utilisation rate calculations.
  - All timestamps are recorded in local time at origin airport.

Known Limitations:
  - Crew assignment data covers a representative sample, not all operating crew members.
  - Full load factor analysis is limited by partial passengers_boarded data.
  - Cost impact data is unavailable for ATC restriction events not escalating to formal irregularity records.
  - Weather delay classifications have not been cross-referenced against third-party meteorological sources.
