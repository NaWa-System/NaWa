**Organisation:** ClearPath Health Partners
**Reporting Period:** January 1, 2024 – June 30, 2024
**Report Type:** Operations
**Prepared By:** Analytics & Reporting Team
**Date of Issue:** July 2024

---

## Executive Summary

This operational report examines clinician-level appointment activity, specialty throughput, and scheduling efficiency across ClearPath Health Partners' clinical network during the first half of 2024. Eight clinicians delivered services across five clinic locations spanning five specialty areas. The report provides a basis for evaluating workload distribution, identifying capacity constraints, and informing staffing decisions for H2 2024.

Appointment activity was unequally distributed across clinicians, with cardiology specialists at ClearPath Central and ClearPath Southbrook accounting for disproportionately high revenue due to the high-fee diagnostic services they deliver. Part-time clinicians maintained meaningful caseloads consistent with their contracted availability.

Scheduling efficiency remained high across the network, with no individual clinician recording more than one non-completion event. The four non-completion events (two no-shows, two cancellations) appear to be patient-driven rather than clinician-driven, and proactive appointment reminder protocols are recommended as a mitigation measure.

Specialty mix is well-aligned with the patient demographic: Cardiology and Endocrinology serve the growing chronic disease population, Primary Care maintains the highest volume backbone, and Pediatrics and Orthopedics serve focused patient groups. ClearPath Lakeview (Toledo) is flagged for operational risk due to single-clinician deployment.

## Section 1: Clinician Appointment Volume & Completion Rate

Appointment volumes varied significantly across the eight active clinicians. Full-time Primary Care physicians at ClearPath Central and ClearPath Southbrook maintained the highest appointment counts, while cardiologists contributed strong volumes relative to their specialty. All clinicians achieved high individual completion rates. No single clinician was responsible for more than one non-completed appointment across the period, reflecting consistently professional patient management across the team.



---

## Section 2: Specialty Throughput & Revenue Contribution

Cardiology generated the highest average billed revenue per appointment, driven by cardiac stress tests ($420) and ECGs ($300). Primary Care delivered the highest total appointment volume. Endocrinology contributed meaningful revenue from diabetes management consultations despite being staffed by a single part-time clinician. Pediatrics maintained consistent preventive visit volumes. Orthopedics contributed two high-value specialty appointments. The specialty mix is appropriate for the current patient demographic, though Cardiology and Endocrinology capacity should be monitored as the chronic disease panel grows.



---

## Section 3: No-Show & Cancellation Analysis

Four of 35 scheduled appointments did not result in completed visits — two no-shows and two cancellations, an aggregate non-completion rate of 11.4%. Non-completion events were distributed across different clinicians and specialties, indicating systemic rather than clinician-specific drivers. Patient-side factors such as transportation, competing healthcare needs, or appointment confusion are likely contributors. A structured reminder and rescheduling protocol is recommended, especially for chronic disease patients with complex care needs.



---

## Section 4: Clinic-Level Clinician Deployment

ClearPath Central (Columbus) hosts three clinicians across Primary Care and Cardiology — the largest single-location deployment. ClearPath Southbrook (Cincinnati) and ClearPath Northgate (Cleveland) each operate with two complementary clinicians. ClearPath Westside (Dayton) maintains dedicated Pediatrics coverage. ClearPath Lakeview (Toledo) operates with a single Orthopedics clinician, creating operational risk. Adding at least one Primary Care clinician at Lakeview is recommended to improve patient access, appointment volume, and revenue resilience.



---

## Section 5: Monthly Clinician Activity Trends

Monthly completed appointment volumes were stable and showed modest growth across H1 2024. January and February each saw five completions. March through June maintained six completions per month on average. Month-over-month consistency indicates stable demand and reliable clinical scheduling. Monitoring for individual clinician plateaus should begin in Q3 to detect any early signs of panel saturation or scheduling bottlenecks before they affect patient access.



---

## Section 6: Follow-Up & Recurring Patient Load by Clinician

A significant portion of the appointment load consists of return visits from established patients managing chronic conditions. Cardiologists managing patients with coronary artery disease recorded multiple appointments with the same patients across the six-month period, indicating dense longitudinal caseloads. This recurring revenue base is commercially valuable but may constrain new patient onboarding capacity. Cardiologists and endocrinologists should be monitored for panel saturation as the patient population grows into H2 2024.

## Appendix & Notes

### Assumptions
- Clinician utilisation measured by appointment volume only; available slot capacity not modelled.
- Part-time clinicians assumed at approximately 50% of full-time scheduling availability.
- All completed appointments represent standard-duration clinical encounters.
- Non-completion events attributed to patient-side factors in the absence of clinician schedule data.

### Known Limitations
- Appointment slot capacity not tracked; true utilisation rates cannot be calculated.
- Clinician absence and leave periods are not recorded in the schema.
- Dataset of 35 appointments across 8 clinicians is a small sample for robust individual benchmarking.
- Patient panel size not formally tracked; recurring load is inferred from appointment records.
- No patient satisfaction or quality-of-care metrics are available in the current data.

### Recommended Next Analysis
- Add a scheduling capacity table to enable true utilisation rate calculation per clinician.
- Track absence and leave data to produce adjusted productivity metrics.
- Conduct patient panel size analysis per clinician against recommended thresholds.
- Model revenue impact of adding a second clinician at ClearPath Lakeview.
- Extend analysis to full-year 2024 to identify seasonal demand patterns.
