EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
========================================

This report presents an assessment of flight operations performance across the Meridian Air and Crestline Airways networks for Q1 2024. The analysis covers all operated and cancelled flights, on-time performance (OTP), delay distribution, load factors, and aircraft utilisation across both carriers.

Across the quarter, the combined network operated 28 completed flights out of 30 scheduled departures, yielding a completion rate of 93.3% and a cancellation rate of 6.7%. Both cancellations were isolated incidents, though the rate remains above the regional European industry benchmark of 2–4%.

On-time performance showed clear improvement across the quarter. January recorded the highest concentration of significant delays, with one incident reaching 55 minutes. By March, the majority of completed flights recorded zero delay minutes, indicating improved operational coordination.

Load factors in the economy cabin averaged approximately 83% across the network, with business class bookings concentrated on longer and flexible-fare routes. All six registered aircraft were utilised throughout the quarter with no recorded groundings.

Overall, Q1 2024 demonstrates a resilient operational baseline with clear improvement trends, while flagging the need to address cancellation rates and early-quarter delay patterns ahead of Q2.

REPORT SECTIONS
========================================

1. FLIGHT COMPLETION & CANCELLATION SUMMARY
--------------------------------------------
Of the 30 flights scheduled across Q1 2024, 28 reached completion and 2 were cancelled. The cancelled flights were MA610 (London–Vienna, 30 January) and CL504 (Dublin–Athens, 28 February). Both cancellations resulted in zero revenue but still incurred fixed operational costs including crew standby and airport fees. The 6.7% cancellation rate exceeds the regional European benchmark of 2–4% and is flagged as a priority for Q2 monitoring.




2. ON-TIME PERFORMANCE BY MONTH
---------------------------------
January was the weakest OTP month, with five of eleven scheduled flights recording delays ranging from 8 to 55 minutes. February showed moderate improvement, though a 50-minute delay on CL302 (Dublin–Madrid, 15 February) remained a notable exception. March recorded the strongest performance, with only two delays and none exceeding 37 minutes. The progressive improvement across the quarter is an encouraging trend that should be sustained into Q2.




3. DELAY DISTRIBUTION BY ROUTE
--------------------------------
Route-level analysis reveals that the London–Rome (MA303) and Manchester–Barcelona (MA505) corridors were disproportionately responsible for accumulated delay minutes. London–Rome recorded 55 minutes of delay in January and 12 minutes in March, suggesting persistent challenges at FCO. The Dublin–Athens medium-haul route consistently performed on-time, likely benefiting from more generous scheduled buffers on the longer sector.




4. AIRCRAFT UTILISATION OVERVIEW
----------------------------------
All six aircraft were deployed across the quarter. G-MERB (Airbus A321, 220 seats) was most frequently assigned to high-volume routes such as London–Madrid, providing the highest seat-capacity yield per operation. EI-CLA (Airbus A319, 144 seats) efficiently served the short-haul Dublin–Paris route with lower fuel cost per sector. No groundings were recorded; however, per-flight maintenance costs should be monitored on a rolling basis.




5. LOAD FACTOR ANALYSIS BY CABIN CLASS
----------------------------------------
Economy cabin load factors were consistently strong at 80–95% across most routes. Business class bookings were concentrated on longer routes — particularly Dublin–Athens and London–Rome — and on flexible-fare itineraries. Short-haul routes such as London–Paris recorded minimal business class uptake, consistent with leisure demand patterns. Ancillary revenue tracked proportionally with passenger volumes, with flexible-fare passengers yielding the highest ancillary revenue per booking.




6. OPERATIONAL COST BREAKDOWN
-------------------------------
Fuel costs represent the dominant operating expense across the network. The Dublin–Athens medium-haul service (2,822 km) incurred the highest absolute fuel costs per operation. Cancelled flights still generated crew and airport fee charges, underscoring the financial cost of cancellations beyond just lost revenue. Airport fees were highest for LHR and CDG operations. Crew costs remained stable across all routes, reflecting standardised crew agreements at both carriers.

APPENDIX & NOTES
========================================

ASSUMPTIONS
- Delay minutes are calculated from actual_arrival vs. scheduled_arrival where both values exist.
- Cancelled flights are excluded from OTP and load factor averages but included in cost summaries.
- Seat capacity is taken from the aircraft table and represents total saleable seats per departure.
- Zero-passenger bookings on cancelled flights are excluded from revenue and load factor calculations.

KNOWN LIMITATIONS
- The 30-flight sample provides limited statistical confidence for definitive trend conclusions.
- Delay root cause categories are not captured in the current schema.
- Passenger satisfaction or complaint data is not available in this dataset version.
- Connecting passenger flows are not modelled, which may affect load factor interpretation on hub routes.

RECOMMENDED NEXT ANALYSIS
- Introduce a delay_reason field to the flights table for root cause classification of delay incidents.
- Extend the reporting window to H1 2024 to assess sustainability of March OTP improvements.
- Add a passenger feedback or NPS data table to correlate on-time performance with customer satisfaction.
- Develop a route profitability scoring model combining booking revenue and flight cost data.
