# Consulting Operations & Project Performance Report — Q1–Q2 2024

**Organisation:** Nexlayer Consulting Group  
**Reporting Period:** January 2024 – June 2024  
**Report Type:** Operations  
**Prepared by:** Business Intelligence & Operations Team  
**Date:** June 2024

---

## Executive Summary

This report presents an operational review of Nexlayer Consulting Group's project portfolio, consultant utilisation, and service line performance for the period January through June 2024. During this half-year period, Nexlayer ran a total of twelve active or recently completed client engagements spanning six service lines, reflecting the firm's continued growth in both advisory and technical delivery capacity.

The portfolio mix remains healthy, with a balance between Fixed-Fee and Time-and-Materials engagements distributed across Financial Services, Healthcare, Public Sector, and emerging verticals including Energy and Logistics. Three projects were completed within the reporting period — the AWS Cloud Migration for Hartwell Financial Corp, the Cybersecurity Audit for Pelham Insurance Group, and the Digital Transformation Roadmap for Consolidated Transit Authority — all delivered on schedule based on contracted end dates.

Consultant utilisation is a key focus area this period. Senior and mid-level consultants are operating at or near full capacity, particularly within the Cloud Migration and Security service lines, where demand has outpaced available headcount. Junior consultants show a marginally lower billable ratio, which is expected given onboarding and ramp-up requirements.

Managed Services retainers have continued to grow steadily, with seven active retainers contributing approximately $54,700 in predictable monthly recurring revenue. Overall, the operational outlook for H2 2024 is positive, contingent on timely recruitment of additional cloud and security specialists.

## 1. Project Portfolio Overview

Nexlayer's H1 2024 portfolio comprised twelve projects across six service lines and ten distinct clients. The portfolio is broadly split between Fixed-Fee engagements (eight projects) and Time-and-Materials contracts (four projects). Fixed-Fee work provides revenue certainty, while Time-and-Materials contracts offer flexibility for scope-evolving programmes.

The three completions during the period — Cloud Migration Phase 1, Cybersecurity Audit, and Digital Transformation Roadmap — collectively represent approximately $525,000 in contracted value delivered. Nine projects remain active, with end dates scheduled through Q3 and Q4 2024, providing a healthy backlog heading into the second half of the year.

---

## 2. Consultant Utilisation Analysis

Across all timesheet records submitted for H1 2024, Nexlayer consultants logged a combined total of over 1,900 billable hours. The firm's overall billable utilisation rate stands at approximately 88.6%, indicating strong demand relative to available capacity.

Senior consultants — particularly Aaron Bellamy (Cloud Architecture) and James Okonkwo (Security) — are assigned across multiple concurrent projects, raising a flag for resource concentration risk. Mid-level consultants maintain consistent utilisation, while junior consultants such as Cleo Hartmann and Rachel Chen are ramping effectively with utilisation improving month-over-month.

Non-billable time is primarily attributable to internal training, bid support, and project onboarding activities.

---

## 3. Service Line Performance

Cloud Migration & Infrastructure remains the firm's highest-volume service line by number of projects (four engagements), driven by continued enterprise cloud adoption. Cybersecurity & Compliance is the highest day-rate service line ($1,950/day) and has performed strongly with two concurrent projects in H1.

Data Analytics & BI has grown meaningfully with three active projects, reflecting client demand for insight-driven decision-making. Digital Transformation Strategy saw one completion and no new project start in the period, suggesting a need for targeted pipeline development in this high-margin advisory line.

---

## 4. Client Portfolio & Account Distribution

Hartwell Financial Corp is Nexlayer's deepest engagement client in H1 2024, holding both a completed cloud migration project and an active data warehouse build, plus an ongoing managed services retainer. Meridian Health Systems similarly holds two concurrent active engagements, making it the second-most significant account by contracted value.

The Public Sector accounts — Consolidated Transit Authority and Keystone Municipal Services — represent steady, lower-risk revenue. BrightPath Diagnostics and Crestview Community College are newer clients representing pipeline development into Healthcare and Education. Regional distribution is weighted toward the Northeast and Southwest, with West Coast representation growing.

---

## 5. Managed Services Retainer Trends

Managed Services retainers provide Nexlayer with approximately $54,700 per month in recurring revenue from seven active retainers. The addition of Riverstone Energy Partners and Summit Logistics Inc as retainer clients in 2024 demonstrates the firm's ability to convert project engagements into long-term managed relationships.

The cancellation of the Aldgate Capital Markets retainer in March 2024 reduced monthly recurring revenue by $4,900 but was anticipated given that client's inactive account status. Average retainer value is approximately $7,814 per month, with Meridian Health Systems holding the largest retainer at $11,200/month.

---

## 6. Resource Allocation & Capacity Risk

An analysis of project assignments reveals three senior consultants — Aaron Bellamy, James Okonkwo, and Fiona Marsh — assigned to multiple simultaneous projects. Aaron Bellamy in particular holds concurrent assignments on three engagements, creating a dependency risk for both in-flight and upcoming projects.

The firm's junior cloud engineering capacity is insufficient to absorb growing demand. Recruitment of at least two mid-level cloud engineers and one additional security analyst before Q3 2024 is recommended.

## Appendix & Notes

### Assumptions
- Utilisation rates are calculated using total hours recorded in the timesheets table only.
- Monthly recurring revenue from retainers is based on the monthly_fee field and does not account for mid-month changes.
- Project status classifications are taken directly from the projects table as of report generation date.
- Contracted value figures represent total agreed contract value, not revenue recognised to date.

### Known Limitations
- Timesheet data does not capture expense claims or travel costs.
- No project milestone or deliverable table exists, limiting schedule adherence analysis.
- Non-billable hours are not categorised by reason.
- Consultant leave records are not captured, so capacity calculations assume full availability.

### Recommended Next Analysis
- Develop a project profitability model combining billable hours, consultant costs, and invoiced revenue.
- Introduce timesheet reason codes for non-billable hours.
- Build a forward capacity model overlaying assignments with pipeline opportunities.
- Conduct a client retention analysis linking retainer tenure and engagement history.
