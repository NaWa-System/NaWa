Q1 2024 REVENUE & INVOICE COLLECTIONS REPORT
SwiftRoute Logistics
Reporting Period: January 1 - March 31, 2024
Report Type: Finance
Prepared By: Finance & Billing Team

========================================================
EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
========================================================

SwiftRoute Logistics generated meaningful freight revenue across all three months of Q1 2024, with invoice totals rising progressively from January through March as shipment volumes increased. January and February invoices have been fully collected, reflecting a healthy payment culture among long-standing retainer and per-shipment clients alike. March invoices, issued at the end of the month with standard 14-day payment terms, show a mixed collections picture: two accounts fully paid, two outstanding, and one partially settled.

Total invoiced revenue across the quarter reached approximately $17,223, combining freight charges and fuel surcharges billed to eight active customers. The March invoice cycle accounts for the largest share of outstanding receivables, driven primarily by Greenfield Manufacturing and BlueSky Apparel -- both per-shipment clients without retainer credit structures. These two accounts represent the primary near-term collections risk entering Q2.

Fuel surcharges, calculated as a consistent percentage add-on to base freight charges, contributed reliably across all shipments and regions. Retainer clients demonstrated strong and predictable payment behaviour throughout the quarter, reinforcing the strategic value of the retainer account model for revenue stability and cash flow predictability.

Management is advised to prioritise collections outreach to Greenfield Manufacturing (approximately $4,103 outstanding) and BlueSky Apparel (approximately $451 outstanding) as Q2 begins. The partial balance from Harborline Retail Co. should be resolved within the next billing cycle. Overall, the company's revenue trajectory is positive and Q1 collections performance for January and February is commendable.

========================================================
SECTION 1: MONTHLY INVOICED REVENUE OVERVIEW
========================================================

Invoice totals grew month over month across Q1 2024. January invoicing totalled approximately $5,027, driven by a broad spread of shipments across all four regions. February invoices reached approximately $4,390, with a slight reduction attributable to fewer high-value Express shipments in that billing cycle. March recorded the highest monthly total at approximately $7,806, reflecting the surge in shipment activity in the final weeks of the quarter. This growth trajectory is encouraging, though the concentration of outstanding balances in March introduces short-term cash flow variability that management should monitor closely.


Data Fields Used: invoices.invoice_date, invoices.total_amount, invoices.paid_amount, invoices.status

========================================================
SECTION 2: INVOICE COLLECTIONS & PAYMENT STATUS
========================================================

January and February invoices were 100% collected across all billed customers. Of the eight March invoices issued, two were fully paid (Apex Wholesale Distributors and NorthEast Medical Supplies), one is partially settled (Harborline Retail Co. with approximately $555 outstanding), and three are fully outstanding: Greenfield Manufacturing at approximately $4,103, BlueSky Apparel at approximately $451, and FreshMart Grocery Chain at approximately $390.50. The two outstanding per-shipment accounts represent the greatest near-term collections exposure and should be escalated before their April 14 due dates.


Data Fields Used: invoices.status, invoices.total_amount, invoices.paid_amount, invoices.due_date, invoices.customer_id, customers.company_name

========================================================
SECTION 3: REVENUE BY CUSTOMER ACCOUNT
========================================================

Greenfield Manufacturing was the highest-revenue customer in Q1, driven by large freight volumes on the Mid-Atlantic corridor including multiple Semi-Trailer loads. Apex Wholesale Distributors and Castleton Electronics followed as consistent revenue contributors under their retainer agreements. Harborline Retail Co. generated steady, predictable revenue across all three months. Smaller per-shipment clients such as FreshMart Grocery Chain, BlueSky Apparel, and Pinecrest Home Goods contributed incrementally and show growth potential as their shipment frequency increases. NorthEast Medical Supplies remained a reliable mid-tier retainer contributor throughout the quarter.


Data Fields Used: invoices.customer_id, invoices.total_amount, customers.company_name, customers.account_type, shipments.freight_charge, shipments.fuel_surcharge

========================================================
SECTION 4: FREIGHT CHARGE VS. FUEL SURCHARGE BREAKDOWN
========================================================

Across all 30 Q1 shipments, freight charges constituted approximately 91% of total billed amounts, with fuel surcharges making up the remaining approximately 9%. The consistent surcharge rate ensures partial recovery of fuel cost exposure across all client types and service tiers. The Mid-Atlantic region generated the highest absolute fuel surcharge revenue due to the weight and distance of its shipments. Express service shipments in the Greater NYC corridor produced the highest per-shipment surcharge values relative to their freight base charge.


Data Fields Used: shipments.freight_charge, shipments.fuel_surcharge, shipments.region_id, shipments.service_type, regions.region_name

========================================================
SECTION 5: REVENUE BY SERVICE TYPE
========================================================

Express shipments generated the highest average freight charge per job, making them the most financially valuable service tier on a per-shipment basis despite representing only one-third of total volume. Standard shipments drove the largest aggregate share of total revenue by count, as they were the most frequently used tier across all customer segments. Same-Day shipments, while limited to short-haul urban routes, yielded moderate freight charges and represent a key differentiator in the Greater NYC market. Service type mix should be actively managed to maximise revenue yield per delivery asset deployed throughout the network.


Data Fields Used: shipments.service_type, shipments.freight_charge, shipments.fuel_surcharge, shipments.weight_kg

========================================================
SECTION 6: ACCOUNT TYPE PERFORMANCE - RETAINER VS. PER-SHIPMENT
========================================================

Retainer accounts provided predictable invoice cadence and demonstrated zero payment defaults throughout Q1 2024. Per-shipment accounts generated higher individual invoice variability and introduced the bulk of the quarter's outstanding receivables risk. The collections data reinforces the strategic value of growing the retainer client base. Retainer accounts also tend to produce multi-shipment invoices that smooth revenue recognition across the billing cycle and reduce the administrative burden associated with collections follow-up.


Data Fields Used: customers.account_type, invoices.total_amount, invoices.paid_amount, invoices.status, customers.customer_id, customers.company_name

APPENDIX
========================================================

ASSUMPTIONS
- Invoice totals reflect freight charges and fuel surcharges as recorded in the shipments table, and may include minor rounding differences from the invoice_shipments join.
- All January and February invoices with status Paid are assumed to have been settled within their 14-day payment terms.
- The partial payment on invoice 16 (Harborline Retail Co.) is assumed to have been received in good faith, with the balance expected within the extended due window.
- Outstanding invoices are not yet overdue as of the report preparation date, given the April 14 due dates.
- Fuel surcharge is assumed to be approximately 10% of the base freight charge across all shipments.

KNOWN LIMITATIONS
- No payment transactions table exists in the current schema; payment dates and partial payment histories cannot be reconstructed at a granular level.
- Individually negotiated credit terms for retainer clients are not captured in the database.
- Operational costs such as driver wages, fuel expenditure, and vehicle leasing fees are not tracked, preventing gross margin or profitability analysis.
- In-transit and delayed March shipments are included in billed invoice amounts and may require credit adjustments if services remain incomplete.

RECOMMENDED NEXT ANALYSIS
- Introduce a payment_transactions table to track individual receipts and enable aging analysis and Days Sales Outstanding (DSO) calculation.
- Add a cost_records table capturing fuel costs, driver wages, and vehicle expenses per shipment to enable contribution margin reporting.
- Conduct a receivables aging analysis in April once March invoice due dates have passed to assess whether outstanding accounts have settled or require escalation.
- Model the revenue impact of converting BlueSky Apparel and FreshMart Grocery Chain from per-shipment to retainer account structures based on their Q1 shipment frequency and average invoice value.
